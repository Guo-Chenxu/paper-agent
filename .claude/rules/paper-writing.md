# Paper Writing Rules

## Title

- Make the title strong and memorable.
- Use as few modifiers as possible.
- Mention the system name repeatedly throughout the paper.
- Be careful with common modifiers whose intended meaning may differ from their standard definition.

## Abstract

- The abstract should be a highly condensed version of the entire paper.
- It should still cover the background, problem, and solution.
- Emphasize the main contributions. A clear structure can be: "First, ... Second, ... Third, ..."

## Introduction

- The introduction should serve as a condensed version of the whole paper.
- The introduction should contain rich content, and its structure should align with the later sections of the paper.
- Repeatedly describe the advantages of the design.
- Cite prior work and clearly state its limitations.
- The contribution section should cover the full paper and explain each contribution clearly.

## Related Work

- Keep the related work section concise.
- Organize and present related work according to the paper's contributions.
- Keep the indexing structure and paragraph structure consistent.

## Writing and Wording

- Avoid overly long sentences. Use fewer complex sentences, and avoid chaining more than three clauses. If a sentence is too long, split it into shorter sentences while preserving smooth transitions.
- Avoid vague modifiers, especially words such as "may" and "usually." Use simple sentences to express the intended meaning directly.
- Use fewer `-ing` forms, and avoid perfect tenses.
- Use "we" less often. Use passive voice when it helps emphasize the paper's main subject.
- Keep terminology consistent throughout the paper.
- Revise sentences that are grammatically correct but not idiomatic or clear.
- Use formal, written academic wording. Avoid colloquial expressions, conversational phrasing, and informal contractions.
- Avoid stacking more than three nouns. When necessary, use prepositions such as "of" and "for" to split noun phrases.
- Countable nouns should either take an article such as "a" or appear in plural form.
- Avoid repeating the same word too often. Use appropriate synonyms when possible.
- Each paragraph should express only one main idea. A topic-first structure is preferred: the first sentence summarizes the paragraph's main point, and the following sentences elaborate on it.
- Do not use two sentences to express the same meaning. Do not repeat information that has already been stated.
- Use precise wording. Some words have both broad and narrow meanings, so choose the meaning carefully. For example, "heterogeneous" usually refers to settings such as CPU-GPU heterogeneity.

## Formatting

- Subfigures also need subtitles. A short explanation can follow the figure title, and the caption should explain what the figure means.
- Use grayscale figures and avoid color when possible.
- The paper should be rich with visuals alongside text. Use figures, tables, diagrams, architecture overviews, and workflow illustrations to break up dense prose. A paper that is pure text with few or no visuals is tedious to read. Aim for roughly one visual element (figure, table, or diagram) per 1-2 pages. Visuals should not just decorate — each must convey information that would be cumbersome to describe in text alone.
