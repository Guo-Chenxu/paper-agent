# Conclusion Writing Guide

## Goal

Close the paper with clear takeaways and credible limitations.

## Structure

1. Restate the problem and core contribution.
2. Summarize the strongest evidence for the contribution.
3. State practical, theoretical, empirical, methodological, or community impact.
4. Add a limitation paragraph.
5. End with a concrete future direction.

## Limitation Guidance

Prefer limitations tied to contribution boundaries, for example:

1. Assumptions required by the analysis, system, study, proof, model, or artifact.
2. Threat model boundaries for security, privacy, reliability, or adversarial settings.
3. Proof scope, formal model restrictions, or unproven cases.
4. Study population, participant context, task design, or ecological validity limits.
5. Measurement bias, instrumentation limits, sampling limits, or confounding factors.
6. Deployment setting, operational constraints, hardware/software dependencies, or organizational context.
7. Workload coverage, input class, data regime, scale, environment, or scenario boundaries.

Avoid framing the conclusion around fixable implementation flaws unless they critically define the contribution's scope.

## Distinguish Limitation Types

1. Technical, study, proof, or analysis defect: a flaw that undermines the main claim or causes an unacceptable tradeoff.
2. Scope limitation: a boundary on where the contribution has been shown, proven, measured, deployed, or studied.
3. Assumption limitation: a condition that must hold for the contribution or evidence to apply.

## Template

1. This paper addresses [problem] with [contribution].
2. The key idea is [core insight], which enables [main benefit].
3. Evidence shows [main supported takeaway] in/for [validated scope].
4. A current limitation is [assumption/scope/validity boundary], and extending to [future setting] is an important next step.
